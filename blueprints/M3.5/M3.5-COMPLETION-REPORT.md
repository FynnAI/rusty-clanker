# M3.5 Completion Report — Verification Hardening (PLAN-D9)

Milestone record for M3.5 (blueprints M3.5-B01–B06, the field-report
mini-wave, and the close-out corrections the manager's final audit forced),
covering the commit range `7edc0aa` (M3 complete) through the final M3.5
commit on `main`. Roadmap criteria: `docs/planning/11-roadmap-milestones.md`,
"M3.5 — Verification Hardening".

**Status: criteria 1, 3, 4 and 5 are green on the final main; criterion 2 is
recorded in §1 exactly as far as the first real scheduled `protocol-diff` run
got.** Every M3.5 blueprint went through the TEST-D57 reference-verification
gate before implementation — the gate's first real use (§3). The close-out
also surfaced two verification-infrastructure defects older than M3.5 that
this report records because they change what "green" has meant so far (§1,
criterion 5): the scheduled CI tier had never once run past its first step,
and the M2 save-cadence acceptance leg could not pass by construction.

### Criterion 2 — protocol-differential harness, first scheduled run — **OPEN, see below**

- The harness itself is complete and real-run-verified on the "ours" side
  (M3.5-B03): packet-recorder relay, scripted TEST-D54 session including the
  survival dig, all 51 redstone contraptions over the wire, structural
  normalization (KeepAlive, Login seed, teleport/entity ids, SetTime),
  byte-level per-packet-type multiset diff, scheduled CI job, and the gated
  `--debug-hooks` server flag with its own acceptance tests.
- The oracle side never completed on this development machine (four real
  attempts, each past the raised 3300 s subprocess deadline under build
  contention), so the first real oracle-vs-ours diff had to come from the
  uncontended CI runners (`workflow_dispatch` 33680590475).
- `windows-2025`: no diff. The oracle capture ran 21:05Z–22:00Z into its
  3300 s deadline, then the "ours" capture ran 22:00Z–22:50Z into its 3000 s
  deadline — sequentially, 2 h 11 min for the step, and the runner prints no
  per-step progress, so how far either side got is unknown. The "ours" side
  had completed locally on this 8-core machine during B03; the 2-core runner
  is at least ~2× slower than the budgets assumed.
- `ubuntu-24.04`: never returned. The step was still running after four
  hours, past both deadlines, because `spawn_drained`'s timeout path killed
  only the direct child (`cargo run`) and then blocked on the pipes that the
  runner binary and the oracle JVM it had spawned still held open. Fixed on
  the final main (process-group tree kill, bounded post-kill drain, a test
  that restates the orphaned-grandchild case); the four dispatched runs were
  cancelled once their acceptance jobs had reported. The Linux leg therefore
  produced no timing evidence of its own beyond "longer than Windows".
- **First real diff (2026-09-03, run 33736929221, TEST-D58 shape).** With
  per-step timings, tree-kill and the parallel job shape, both captures on
  both runners finished inside 5400 s and the diff jobs produced the first
  oracle-vs-ours report. The 32 session steps all captured (49 s of real
  work); the 51 contraptions did not — `redstone_wire_capture` walked the bot
  toward a `y = 4` origin floating 64 blocks above both worlds' surface, so
  every contraption burned the 90 s walk timeout (51 × 90 s ≈ the whole
  77 min a capture took) and the capture case still passed on 0 of 51. Both
  defects are fixed in the follow-up governance changeset (floor-relative
  slots near spawn, pass only on 51/51). The session diff itself: 32 of 32
  steps diverge, every divergence a whole packet type the oracle sends and we
  do not (`set_time`; `set_entity_motion`, `move_entity_*`, `rotate_head`,
  `set_entity_data`, `add_entity` for the item drop of every break; chunk
  streaming and `chunk_batch_*` pacing on movement; `game_event` on the
  gamemode switch; and the join sequence: `change_difficulty`, `commands`,
  `container_set_content`, `initialize_border`, `player_abilities`,
  `player_info_update`, `recipe_book_*`, `server_data`, `set_experience`,
  `set_held_slot`, `ticking_state`/`ticking_step`, `update_advancements`,
  `update_attributes`, `update_recipes`, `system_chat`), plus four body
  differences: the brand payload (`rusty-clanker` vs `vanilla`, by design),
  the `dialog` registry-data entry of the configuration phase,
  `login_finished`'s trailing session UUID (random per session — a normalizer
  gap, verified against the reference), and the dug block's Y one below ours
  (the vanilla flat preset's surface is one layer lower than our placeholder
  world's). The two runners differ only in timer-driven presence (`set_time`,
  `keep_alive`, drop-entity motion), which is what TEST-D59's `Timer` class
  exists for.
- Disposition (TEST-D59): the inventory above became the known-divergence
  register (81 entries, hash-manifested, path-guard protected), resolved by
  the diff job; `Timer` entries are permanent, every `Missing`/`Body` entry
  names the milestone that closes it and fails the run once that milestone's
  completion report exists.
- **Second real run (2026-09-03, run 33789270683 — parallel jobs, flat-preset
  world, floor-relative contraption slots, register).** All four captures
  finished inside budget with 51 of 51 contraptions and 32 of 32 steps
  (`ubuntu-24.04` "ours": 11.6 min); the diff jobs produced a 66 KB report
  instead of 135 MB. 18 of 87 cases pass, among them fourteen session steps
  the register now resolves (every break/place of chests, furnaces, hoppers,
  smokers, pistons; the survival dig; sneak). The remaining failures are,
  after decoding the packet bodies locally: (a) the register's own scoping —
  its entries were written for `session/*` and never matched a
  `redstone/<category>/<name>` step, and the login/configuration steps'
  packets resolved to no name at all; (b) harness asymmetries, not redstone
  divergences — the oracle's default view distance 10 sends the tick-barrier
  marker block ninety blocks away where our radius 5 does not, and setup
  writes the oracle spreads over several ticks arrive as single
  `block_update`s while ours coalesce into one `section_blocks_update`; (c)
  four genuine product gaps surfaced by the contraptions: no
  `block_event` packets (pistons), no `set_equipment`, no
  `player_info_remove`, and the join-sequence bodies already registered.
  The corpus also holds one physically invalid fixture
  (`comparator_container_fullness_chest` has no floor, so the vanilla oracle
  pops the comparator one tick after setup — the source of the recurring
  `m3-acceptance` red on `ubuntu-24.04`). The follow-up governance changeset
  in flight adds a per-contraption observation window (packets after setup,
  block changes inside the bounding box), aligns the oracle's view distance
  with ours for captures, resolves names per step state, widens the
  register, and gives the fixture its floor. Criterion 2 closes on the first
  run after it that reports no unregistered divergence.

### Criterion 5 — ledger backlog and CI — **PASS, with two older defects found and fixed**

- `SectionBlocksUpdate` (0x54) coalescing for ≥2 same-tick changes per
  section, `BlockUpdate` otherwise, with per-viewer view-distance filtering
  via each player's sent-chunk set (bystander outside view distance receives
  nothing; the 20-bot load smoke stays green).
- Hopper `ENABLED` re-evaluated on neighbor change (`HopperBehavior`), and
  the Stage-7 tick honors it.
- Every piped `xtask` subprocess now goes through
  `xtask::process::spawn_drained` — including `m3_5_be_report.rs`, which the
  final audit found still on the poll-then-drain pattern — and `lint-tests`
  rejects raw `Stdio::piped()` under `xtask/src` both per commit and over the
  whole tree (the whole-tree form is what would have caught the survivor).
- Corpus fixture prose batch applied and re-hashed; the two `cargo test`-only
  flakes in xtask (env-var race, shared temp path) fixed.
- Tier-1 CI green on every push of the integration sequence.
- **Scheduled tier, found never to have run.** Every scheduled run in the
  retained history (2026-08-27 onward) failed at `rc-paritybot test` because
  all four nightly jobs ran the paritybot integration tests before building
  the release server they spawn; `m1`/`m2`/`m3-acceptance` had therefore never
  executed in CI. Fixed (build step first); first real results on the final
  main: `m1-acceptance` green on both runners, `m3-acceptance` green on
  `windows-2025` in three of three runs (52/52 with its own fresh capture,
  20-bot load leg), and on `ubuntu-24.04` green in one of three runs and red
  in the other two on one or two contraptions each, every mismatch on the
  captured side (transient tick-0 states, a one-tick-late piston retract) — a
  capture-side tick-alignment looseness on the Linux runner, recorded in the
  ledger with the fix shape TEST-D48 allows; `soak` green on both.
- **M2 save-cadence leg (AC3), found unable to pass by construction.** The
  M2-B08 harness re-ran a five-action bot script every 5 s to "keep a chunk
  dirty", while the server saves a chunk only when it is dirty and the interval
  has elapsed (dirty cleared on save), so the one chunk ever re-saved showed
  gaps of ~150 and ~25 ticks — deterministic on the current main and on the
  M3-final commit `7edc0aa` alike (bisected with a scratch build), locally and
  on both runners. The harness now keeps one block toggling every 250 ms
  through a single connected bot (`restart_persistence_runner churn`), and
  the analyzer treats a chunk's first save gap as warm-up (it is decided by
  when the bot first touched the chunk — 23 ticks on the Linux runner, under
  20 locally — not by the save timer; every later gap is on cadence).
  `m2-report --mode smoke` passes all five cases three times locally and, for
  the first time in CI, on `windows-2025` (run 33690687600) and on both
  runners (run 33692266229).

## 3. The TEST-D57 gate's first use

The research pass over the six blueprints' claims lists produced six
`*-CLAIMS.md` files (B01 13 rows, B02 6, B03 4, B04 exempt, B05 3, B06 6) and
four WRONG rows describing five wrong assumptions, each corrected in the
blueprint before implementation: the replaceable-block list (8 real tag
members missing), byte-offset packet masking (impossible — masking is
structural) together with 26.2's `SetTime` shape, the furnace NBT key names,
and the `lock` field's real shape. One further claim (the chest id range) had
been inverted by the blueprint author and was caught by the planning review
against `blocks.json`.

## 4. Process record

- Every commit carries a valid `Changeset-Type` trailer; per-commit path guard
  and lint-tests green on every push range. One agent's implementation commit
  that had touched test files was split before push (test-authoring first).
- The manager's final review ran as a workflow: one read-only auditor per
  blueprint (130 deliverables checked), three lens-diverse adversarial
  refuters per finding, one fact-checker over this report. Eight of eleven
  findings survived and every one was acted on: two missing B02 ledger entries
  written, the inert claims gate redesigned and re-implemented, the surviving
  raw pipe drained and the lint widened, an unrecorded B04 deviation recorded,
  and this report's overclaims (a protocol-diff result that did not exist,
  universal pipe-drain coverage, "five WRONG verdicts") removed. Three
  findings were refuted as already-recorded, deliberately bounded B03
  simplifications (`SetTime` map keys, real-placement orientation coverage,
  `classify_cell`'s upstream-source qualifier).
- Machine constraints shaped the cadence: ~15.5 GB RAM (two concurrent heavy
  builds OOM under the fat-LTO release profile) and a disk that hit 100% three
  times — worktree targets are wiped as soon as their agent finishes.
- Blueprint-vs-reality mismatches found by implementers (nextest `ci`
  profile, `replay.rs` range count, `verify-claims` heading grammar, runner
  cold-build deadlines) are recorded in the findings ledger.
