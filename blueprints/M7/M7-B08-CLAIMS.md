| The port/firewall matrix (Context §F) names TCP port `25565` (operator-configurable via `--bind`) as carrying "Player connections (Java protocol, NET-D1)", inherited from monolithic mode's own default (M6-B07 §C step 14). | `SharedConstants.DEFAULT_MINECRAFT_PORT = 25565` (SharedConstants.java:113) and `DedicatedServerProperties.serverPort = this.get("server-port", 25565)` (DedicatedServerProperties.java:72). The listener is TCP: `ServerConnectionListener.startTcpServerListener` builds a Netty `ServerBootstrap` on the event-loop group's server socket channel, sets `ChannelOption.TCP_NODELAY` on every accepted child channel and binds `localAddress(address, port)` (ServerConnectionListener.java:59-92). | The comment on `default_cluster_save_interval_ticks() -> u64 { 600 } // CLUSTER-D17: 30s @ 20 TPS` (Context §B) treats 600 ticks as equal to 30 real-time seconds at Minecraft's tick rate of 20 ticks per second. | `SharedConstants.TICKS_PER_SECOND = 20`, `MILLIS_PER_TICK = 50` and `TICKS_PER_MINUTE = 1200` (SharedConstants.java:138-140) — 600 ticks x 50 ms = 30000 ms = 30 s, and 600 is exactly half of the declared ticks-per-minute constant. The nominal tick rate is 20 TPS as the blueprint states; vanilla's own tick loop can run slower than nominal under load or be rescaled by the `/tick rate` command, so 600 ticks is 30 s of nominal, not guaranteed wall-clock, time — the same qualifier every tick-denominated budget in this corpus carries. | CONFIRMED | research role (TEST-D57 pass, claude-opus-5) | 2026-09-05 |
| Context §C step 4's storage-attach probe treats chunk coordinate `x = i32::MIN, z = i32::MIN` as one no real chunk in vanilla 26.2 ever occupies, since vanilla's own world-border-bounded chunk-coordinate range never reaches the `i32` extremes. | Confirmed, though the binding bound is the level-size limit rather than the world border. Horizontal block coordinates are clamped to `[-30000000, 30000000)` by `Level.isInWorldBoundsHorizontal` (Level.java:184-186) against `Level.MAX_LEVEL_SIZE = 30000000` (Level.java:98), giving a real-chunk range of roughly +/-1875000; the world border is the tighter of the two, `WorldBorder.MAX_SIZE = 5.999997E7` diameter with `absoluteMaxSize = 29999984` (WorldBorder.java:22-38). Vanilla's own chunk-validity predicate is `ChunkPos.isValid(x, z) = Mth.absMax(x, z) <= ChunkPyramid.MAX_CHUNK_COORDINATE_VALUE` (ChunkPos.java:68-70), where that ceiling is `blockToSectionCoord(BlockPos.MAX_HORIZONTAL_COORDINATE) - SAFETY_MARGIN_CHUNKS` = 2097151 minus a small margin (ChunkPyramid.java:57-58, BlockPos.java:48/56) — still three orders of magnitude short of `i32::MIN`. Vanilla's own sentinel for "no chunk here" sits in the same safe band: `ChunkPos.INVALID_CHUNK_POS = pack(1875066, 1875066)` (ChunkPos.java:33). One quirk worth knowing before writing a test against the predicate: `Mth.absMax(int, int)` is `max(abs(a), abs(b))` (Mth.java:129-131), and Java's `Math.abs(Integer.MIN_VALUE)` overflows back to `Integer.MIN_VALUE`, so `ChunkPos.isValid(i32::MIN, i32::MIN)` returns true by overflow — the coordinate is still one no real chunk occupies, but it is not one vanilla's validity check rejects. | CONFIRMED | research role (TEST-D57 pass, claude-opus-5) | 2026-09-05 |
